## 英文原文

来源：[Marco Rubio has unique power to promote peace, but he hasn't. He is almost as bad for the world as Trump](https://www.theguardian.com/commentisfree/2026/aug/16/marco-rubio-power-peace-world-us-diplomat)；The Guardian，Simon Tisdall，2026 年 8 月 16 日。

[1] As chief US diplomat, Rubio could do great good. His pandering and neglect are a disgrace we shouldn't ignore

[2] Viewed from the state department in Washington DC, the world is on fire – but record heatwaves are not to blame. The unresolved Iran war has badly burned Donald Trump. His military campaign [conclusively failed](https://www.theguardian.com/commentisfree/2026/jul/18/donald-trump-iran-world-danger-bombing-us), energy and food prices are still rising, and a Republican rout in November's midterm elections looms. Humiliatingly, Israel this week [flatly rejected](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/aug/09/israel-rejects-us-led-15-point-gaza-peace-plan-says-netanyahu) his latest Gaza peace proposals, days after Trump congratulated himself on a "historic breakthrough". In Europe, Russia's [attacks on Nato allies](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/aug/10/us-intelligence-russia-leipzig-airport-drone-bomb) grow increasingly brazen, threatening a wider escalation. The Ukraine war, which Trump vowed to end, is now well into its fifth year, with civilians on both sides caught in the killing fields. And these are just the bigger firestorms. There are plenty more.

[3] Rarely has the need for effective, professional diplomacy been more pressing. So step forward Marco Rubio, US secretary of state and chief American diplomat. These are challenges that, if successfully tackled, could propel the 55-year-old son of Cuban immigrants and the former US senator for Florida into pole position in the 2028 Republican presidential nomination race. Here is a chance for Rubio to defy critics who dismiss him as a lickspittle and Trump dogsbody, and transform himself into respected global statesman. His boss [mocked him](https://time.com/4242827/donald-trump-marco-rubio-insults/) as "little Marco" and a "loser" when they were election rivals in 2016. (Rubio called him a conman.) Now is Rubio's opportunity to go big, be brave and prove he's a winner.

[4] Or maybe not.

[5] Rubio is unlike recent US secretaries of state – heavyweights such as Hillary Clinton and Antony Blinken, or John Kerry, famed for his high-energy shuttle diplomacy. Rubio has little time for "peace processes". Instead, he projects brute US power to impose take-it-or-leave-it "solutions" that advance US interests, as defined by Trump. It's a modern form of imperialism. Target countries have a choice: [become client states](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/03/14/us/politics/rubio-trump-iran-war.html), like Venezuela and, prospectively, Cuba and Greenland, or be crushed. In Iran, the policy is not regime change, let alone democratic renewal. It's regime compliance. Rubio behaves less like a peace-broker, more like a pith-helmeted colonial viceroy demanding fealty from obsequious satraps.

[6] Speaking in Munich in February, Rubio aired his hawkishly retro doctrine of US primacy. He [lamented the decline](https://www.state.gov/releases/office-of-the-spokesperson/2026/02/secretary-of-state-marco-rubio-at-the-munich-security-conference) of "the great western empires … accelerated by godless communist revolutions and anti-colonial uprisings" and warned Europe faced "civilisational erasure". The US didn't want milksop allies "shackled by guilt and shame" about their imperial past, he said. "We want allies who are proud of their culture and heritage … and who, together with us, are willing and able to defend it." Here was a familiar assertion of US hegemony, but one stripped of bogus altruism and pretence. It sounded a death knell for diplomacy. Trump has [attacked eight countries](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/2/28/what-countries-has-trump-attacked-since-returning-to-office) in the past year. Cuba, Rubio's obsession, [could be next](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/05/20/us/politics/marco-rubio-cuba.html).

[7] Rubio's reluctance to get personally involved in peacemaking may be partly explained by his other, contradictory role, as Trump's national security adviser. He has accepted the White House's outsourcing of negotiations over Ukraine and Palestine to amateur envoys of limited ability – namely Trump's son-in-law, Jared Kushner, and business crony Steve Witkoff. Broadly speaking, Rubio has steered clear of direct involvement in the Iran talks, too, leaving JD Vance, the vice-president and his main potential rival in 2028, to take the flak. Rubio's arm's length approach smacks of calculation. Or maybe he's just out of his depth. Maybe he has no clear idea what he, Marco, really thinks about any of these issues and simply does Trump's erratic bidding.

[8] This perception of a political chameleon lacking firm principles, consistent opinions and a strong moral compass is reinforced by his record. His biographer, [Manuel Roig-Franzia](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/06/01/opinion/marco-rubio-florida.html), believes Rubio's personal and political slipperiness – he once became a Mormon, then reverted to Catholicism – is rooted in a childhood need to belong, to be on the winning side, no matter the price. Senator Rubio was a China hawk. Now he [toes Trump's ingratiating line](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/05/16/us/politics/rubio-china-trump-trip.html) on Beijing. He used to castigate Russia; now he's an appeaser. He championed overseas assistance but, in office, has [gutted USAID](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2025/mar-10/marco-rubio-usaid-funding) and seeks to [dismantle the international criminal court](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/jul/13/marco-rubio-dismantle-international-criminal-court), a keystone of global order. He blasted Barack Obama's Iran nuclear deal. Now he backs an accord that would offer additional concessions to Tehran. Accused by the Vatican of waging an immoral, unjust war, he [travelled to Rome](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/may/07/marco-rubio-to-meet-pope-at-the-vatican-after-trump-attacks-on-pontiff) and set the pope straight.

[9] The irony, or tragedy, of the Rubio conundrum is he has the power to do great good, if only he would stand up and be counted. Russia's Vladimir Putin, losing [on the battlefield and at home](https://www.theguardian.com/commentisfree/2026/jun/14/vladimir-putin-ukraine-war-borders-russian-president), is weaker than at any time since 2022. Ukraine's Volodymyr Zelenskyy is [short of missile interceptors](https://www.theguardian.com/commentisfree/2026/aug/14/patriot-missiles-ukraine-putin-kyiv-running-out-nato-danger), soldiers and political support. A ceasefire deal freezing the conflict is there for the making by the only country – the US – that can meaningfully squeeze both sides. Likewise, in Israel, the architect of the Gaza genocide and disastrous wars with Hamas, Iran and Hezbollah – Benjamin Netanyahu – [faces defeat](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/aug/10/benjamin-netanyahu-rejection-us-deal-gaza-risky-gamble) in October's elections. Here's a rare opportunity to quietly shelve Trump's Board of Peace idiocies, halt Jewish settler violence in the West Bank and make a fresh, inclusive start based on UN resolutions.

[10] Rubio could also do the Middle East, the global economy, American consumers and himself a power of good by taking charge of moribund peace talks with Iran and resuscitating the June memorandum of understanding that Tehran insists is the only route out of the strait of Hormuz logjam. In doing so, Rubio would have the enthusiastic backing of most Americans, European and Gulf state governments, energy producers and businesses, and Maga supporters who rightly fear Trump has dragged the US into another forever war. Even Trump may be grateful. He [knows he's in a hole](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/08/10/us/politics/trump-new-strategy-middle-east.html). He needs someone to dig him out.

[11] The fact that Rubio, despite his ostensibly uniquely powerful position, is unlikely to do any of the above marks him out as the most dangerous man in the US administration after Trump himself. He is dangerous because he enables and facilitates Trump's worst instincts – and neglects his duty. He is dangerous because he provides pseudo-intellectual respectability to far-right fantasies about unfettered US supremacy. More so than the impetuous Vance, indulging in puerile name-calling on social media, and the "secretary of war" Pete Hegseth, a ranting ultra-nationalist, Rubio is dangerous because he can look and sound reasonable. He stands a fair chance of becoming [Trump's chosen successor](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/06/03/us/politics/trump-vance-rubio-2028-election.html) and heir to the Maga brand.

[12] Maybe, before that happens, he will change again. Maybe he will rediscover a few principles, find a diplomatic backbone, extinguish a few fires. He has shown repeatedly that his views are infinitely fungible. Rubio could yet do great things. Or he could become a problem as great or greater than Trump.

## 长难句拆解

### 插入条件从句割裂主谓

**原句**：[3] These are challenges that, if successfully tackled, could propel the 55-year-old son of Cuban immigrants and the former US senator for Florida into pole position in the 2028 Republican presidential nomination race.

**主干**：主句 `These are challenges`，后接关系从句 `that ... could propel ... into pole position`；`propel A into B` 是「把 A 推向 B」。

**层次与易错点**：关系从句的主语是 that（即 challenges），谓语 could propel 被插入的条件从句 `if successfully tackled` 隔开；propel 的宾语是一个很长的名词短语，核心词是 son 与 senator，由 and 并列。易错点：看到 `that, if...` 时容易把 if 从句当成主句的条件，从而找不到 could propel 的主语；`successfully tackled` 是省略了 they are 的被动形式，不是主动。

**逻辑关系**：条件 + 结果——如果处理得当，这些挑战会变成机会。

**参考译文**：这些挑战若能妥善应对，足以把他——这位 55 岁的古巴移民之子、佛罗里达州前参议员——推向 2028 年共和党总统提名的领先位置。

**写作迁移**：*These reforms, if properly funded, could move the country closer to energy independence.* 这些改革若资金到位，能让该国更接近能源独立。

### 超长主语与被动谓语

**原句**：[8] This perception of a political chameleon lacking firm principles, consistent opinions and a strong moral compass is reinforced by his record.

**主干**：主语 `This perception`，谓语 `is reinforced by his record`；中间一大串都是 perception 的后置修饰。

**层次与易错点**：`of a political chameleon` 说明是什么样的印象，`lacking firm principles, consistent opinions and a strong moral compass` 是现在分词短语修饰 chameleon，三个并列宾语共用 lacking。易错点：主语与谓语相隔 15 词，读到 lacking 时容易误以为它是谓语；而真正的谓语是被动语态，逻辑是「他的履历加强了这种印象」。

**逻辑关系**：因果（履历 → 印象被强化），被动句把结果放在句首。

**参考译文**：这种认为他是缺乏坚定原则、一致主张和明确道德立场的政治变色龙的印象，因他的过往履历而更加牢固。

**写作迁移**：*This view of the city as a place without a clear identity is reinforced by its scattered planning.* 这种认为该市缺乏清晰定位的看法，因其零散的规划而更加牢固。

### 省略 that 的表语从句

**原句**：[9] The irony, or tragedy, of the Rubio conundrum is he has the power to do great good, if only he would stand up and be counted.

**主干**：主句 `The irony, or tragedy, of the Rubio conundrum is ...`，is 后接表语从句 `he has the power to do great good`，that 被省略。

**层次与易错点**：`or tragedy` 是插入的同位成分，不影响结构；`if only` 引导虚拟语气，表示与事实相反的愿望。易错点：省略 that 后，`is he has` 这样的连续结构会让人误读为倒装疑问句；`if only he would` 不能理解为「只要他愿意就能」，它带有「可惜他没有」的遗憾语气。

**逻辑关系**：转折 + 虚拟愿望——讽刺之处在于他有能力，却没有做。

**参考译文**：卢比奥这一困局的讽刺之处（或者说悲剧之处）在于：他本有能力做成了不起的事——只要他肯公开站出来、承担自己的立场。

**写作迁移**：*The tragedy of the scheme is it could have worked, if only the funding had arrived on time.* 该方案的悲剧在于它本可以奏效，只要资金按时到位。

### The fact that 主语与 despite 插入语叠加

**原句**：[11] The fact that Rubio, despite his ostensibly uniquely powerful position, is unlikely to do any of the above marks him out as the most dangerous man in the US administration after Trump himself.

**主干**：主语是 `The fact that ...`（同位语从句作主语），谓语 `marks him out as ...`。

**层次与易错点**：同位语从句里，Rubio 与 is unlikely 之间插入了 `despite his ostensibly uniquely powerful position`；`ostensibly` 是「表面上」，暗示作者不认可这个「强大」；`marks him out as` 意为「使他显得是」。易错点：把 despite 短语当主句状语会丢掉「按理说权力很大」与「实际上不做」的对照；把 ostensibly 漏译会让句子变成纯褒义。

**逻辑关系**：让步（尽管职位显赫）+ 因果（正因如此才显出危险）。

**参考译文**：卢比奥尽管表面上拥有独一无二的强大职位，却不太可能做上述任何一件事——这一事实使他成为美国政府中除特朗普本人之外最危险的人物。

**写作迁移**：*The fact that the mayor, despite broad public support, has avoided the issue marks her out as a cautious politician.* 市长尽管广受支持却回避这一问题，这一事实使她显得是一个谨慎的政治人物。

## 术语与行业黑话

- **secretary of state**｜本文含义：美国国务卿，即首席外交官｜使用背景：美国内阁首位部长，主管国务院与对外事务｜近词辨析：与 foreign secretary（英国外交大臣）职能相近但国情不同；不等于 national security adviser（总统的国安顾问）｜可否迁移到雅思：可（政治类话题可作具体例证）。
- **regime change**｜本文含义：推翻他国政权｜使用背景：冷战以来美国外交常用说法，本文对比「regime compliance」｜近词辨析：regime change 是换政权，regime compliance 是逼现政权听话，本文认为美国在伊朗采取的是后者｜可否迁移到雅思：可（国际关系话题）。
- **client state**｜本文含义：依附于大国的附庸国｜使用背景：冷战术语，指外交与安全上受大国支配的国家｜近词辨析：client state 有依附关系，ally 是平等盟友｜可否迁移到雅思：可（需说明语境）。
- **hegemony**｜本文含义：霸权，指一国在区域或全球的主导地位｜使用背景：国际关系核心概念，常与「美国霸权」讨论绑定｜近词辨析：hegemony 是结构性主导，dominance 更宽泛｜可否迁移到雅思：可。
- **shuttle diplomacy**｜本文含义：穿梭外交，调解人往返各方之间斡旋｜使用背景：1970 年代基辛格中东斡旋后被广泛使用｜近词辨析：与 mediation 相近，但强调来回奔走的物理动作｜可否迁移到雅思：可（写作中作具体细节）。
- **forever war**｜本文含义：看不到尽头的战争｜使用背景：2001 年后指美国在中东的长期军事行动｜近词辨析：不等于 total war（全面战争），forever war 强调时间无终点｜可否迁移到雅思：可（战争类话题）。
- **Maga**｜本文含义：特朗普「让美国再次伟大」的运动及其支持者｜使用背景：Make America Great Again 的缩写，2016 年起成为政治身份标签｜近词辨析：Maga 指运动与人群，Republican 指政党，两者重叠但不相等｜可否迁移到雅思：不可，除非先交代背景。

## 背景补充

题目：*Some people believe a country should put its own interests above international cooperation. To what extent do you agree or disagree?*

提纲：
- 立场：不完全同意——国家利益是出发点，但长远的国家利益往往依赖合作。
- 主体一：单边施压能短期见效，代价是盟友信任流失。
- 主体二：气候、能源、安全等问题无法由一国单独解决，合作本身就是利益。
- 结论：应把合作视为实现国家利益的手段，而不是与利益对立的选择。

示范段（74 词）：

> Putting national interests first is not inherently unreasonable, since governments answer to their own citizens. However, the argument treats cooperation as a cost rather than a strategy. When a state imposes terms on partners instead of negotiating, it may win a short-term concession while losing the trust that future deals depend on. On issues such as climate and energy, cooperation is not an alternative to national interest; it is how that interest is achieved.

**Part 2**

> **Describe a news story about politics that you found interesting.**
> You should say:
> - what the story was about
> - where you read it
> - what interested you about it
> and explain why you think it matters.

2 分钟提纲（55 词）：`I read a Guardian column about a US secretary of state who had the chance to broker peace but chose pressure instead. What interested me was how the writer argued — not by insulting him, but by showing his contradictions. It matters because it shows how diplomatic language can hide hard power.`

**Part 3**

1. Why do some politicians avoid taking a clear position?（出处：[7]）
   思路：从选举风险与责任归属两点答。
   *Taking a clear position means **owning the outcome**, and that is risky before an election. Staying vague lets them **keep both options open** while others absorb the criticism.*

2. Is it fair for newspapers to publish strong opinions about politicians?（出处：[1]）
   思路：区分事实报道与评论，强调署名与可反驳性。
   *It can be fair, as long as the piece is **clearly labelled as comment** and the writer puts their name to it. Readers can then judge the argument instead of mistaking it for reporting.*

3. How can ordinary readers tell opinion from fact in the news?（出处：[2]）
   思路：看版面归类、看形容词与比喻、查原始来源。
   *A reliable habit is to check whether the article **attributes claims to a named source**. Loaded metaphors and adjectives are another signal that you are reading a view rather than a record.*
