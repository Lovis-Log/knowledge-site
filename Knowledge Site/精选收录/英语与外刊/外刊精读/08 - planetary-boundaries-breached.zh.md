## 英文原文

来源：['Planetary boundaries' necessary for flourishing of life have been massively breached, scientists warn](https://www.theguardian.com/environment/2026/sep/21/planetary-boundaries-breached-scientists-warn-climate-crisis)；The Guardian，Oliver Milman，2026 年 9 月 21 日。

[1] The planetary life support systems relied upon by humans have deteriorated to their worst levels ever recorded amid ongoing pollution and habitat destruction, according to a key yet grim annual update by scientists.

[2] Of the nine "planetary boundaries" that help determine whether life can flourish on Earth, seven are being breached at their highest ever levels of transgression, according to the latest planetary health check, a process that was established in 2009.

[3] The seven key indicators in the danger zone include climate change, change in biosphere integrity, land system change, freshwater change, modification of biogeochemical flows, introduction of novel entities and, [for the first time last year](https://www.theguardian.com/environment/2025/sep/24/worlds-oceans-fail-key-health-check-as-acidity-crosses-critical-threshold-for-marine-life), ocean acidification.

[4] Only two of the assessed areas, stratospheric ozone depletion and atmospheric aerosol loading, have shown improvement over the last decade, although researchers warn that atmospheric aerosols, which can form air pollution, are still a problem in several regions of the world.

[5] The coalition of 60 scientists who conducted [the analysis of the key guardrails](https://publications.pik-potsdam.de/pubman/item/item_35009/component/file_35022/PlanetaryHealthCheck2026.pdf) for human life under the auspices of the Potsdam Institute for Climate Impact Research warned that the consequences of breaching these planetary boundaries is "becoming increasingly clear all around us".

[6] "The diagnosis is unambiguous – pressure is rising across every planetary boundary already outside the safe operating space," said Boris Sakschewski, lead author of the report. "It means that the risk of large-scale, persistent and potentially irreversible change is increasing, while our margin for error in tackling these problems is shrinking."

[7] While there is little time to reverse this parlous situation, humanity does still possess the knowledge and capacity to do so, according to Johan Rockström, a climate scientist and report co-author. "Accelerating action to return to the safe operating space across all planetary boundaries is essential to protect lives and livelihoods, and to deliver on the promise of shared future prosperity," he said.

[8] The assessment, which pored over the latest scientific data, seeks to establish the state of the defined areas that provide a "safe operating space for humanity". Human civilization has developed in the past 10,000 years due to a relatively stable climate and natural resources that sustainably provide us with food, shelter and a safe living environment.

[9] These conditions are being badly warped, however, by the ongoing release of huge amounts of planet-heating pollution, from the burning of fossil fuels, and the mass disposal of other pollution, such as plastic into our environment. Habitats such as forests and wetlands are being torn apart, fragmented and flattened to make way for agriculture and urban areas, imperiling animal species and natural systems upon which we depend.

[10] The ability of landscapes and bodies of water to cope with this and act as a buffer to the worsening climate crisis is "weakening and may be significantly overestimated", the new report warns. While forests and oceans have sucked up a vast amount of the carbon dioxide we have emitted, there are signs that these natural systems are faltering.

[11] For example, there is a chance that the Amazon rainforest, a huge carbon store, could reach a tipping point where it becomes a savannah, which would have "serious implications for climate stability, food and water security, and human health around the world", the report states.

[12] The report comes on the heels of a series of extreme events around the world in the past year that scientists say have been made far more likely by the upending of our environment.

[13] Huge floods hit Pakistan last year, while this year has seen record heat and wildfires across Europe, the hottest ever summer in the US, and [a disastrous flood on the Tibet-Nepal border](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/aug/27/what-caused-the-catastrophic-flash-flood-in-nepal-and-tibet-and-how-does-a-glacier-collapse). The oceans, which are absorbing much of this extra generated heat, have hit new temperature records.

[14] "These are not separate emergencies; the planet is telling us, in every region at once, that it is out of balance," said Hindou Oumarou Ibrahim, chair of Planetary Guardians, a coalition of scientists, business leaders and activists.

[15] "I hear this same story wherever I go, whether from a pastoralist watching the rains fail season after season or a coastal community watching the sea rise. The 2026 planetary health check puts data behind what communities on the frontlines already know from living it. We do not have the luxury of treating these as separate crises any longer."

[16] The report was released on Monday shortly after the start of [Climate Week](https://www.climateweeknyc.org/), a major gathering of politicians, business leaders and activists in New York City that leads up to the UN general assembly.

[17] Members of the Planetary Guardians – including Mary Robinson, the former Irish president; Juan Manuel Santos, the Nobel laureate and ex-president of Colombia; and oceanographer Sylvia Earle – attended the launch of the report. They are part of more than 100,000 attenders expected at Climate Week, which organizers said was set to be the largest ever.

[18] Despite the record numbers attending talks, announcements and other events aimed at tackling the climate crisis, however, the head of the week's non-profit organizer acknowledged a general reticence to talk about global heating.

[19] Helen Clarkson, chief executive of the [Climate Group](https://www.theclimategroup.org/), said that even though the world had been visibly racked by recent climate-fueled disasters, "I've not seen this level of 'green-hushing' before. Many companies tell us they will be here, but not very visibly.

[20] "But after a summer like this when we saw so much tragedy driven by climate change, this is the time to be loud," she added. "The world is standing on the edge of the abyss, and people are seeking change."

## 长难句拆解

### 过去分词后置与介词短语收尾

**原句**：[1] The planetary life support systems relied upon by humans have deteriorated to their worst levels ever recorded amid ongoing pollution and habitat destruction, according to a key yet grim annual update by scientists.

**主干**：主句 `The planetary life support systems ... have deteriorated to their worst levels ever recorded`。

**层次与易错点**：`relied upon by humans` 是过去分词短语修饰 systems；`ever recorded` 修饰 levels；`amid ...` 与句末 `according to ...` 分别交代背景与消息来源。易错点：主语与谓语之间隔着过去分词短语，容易把 relied 误当作谓语；according to 属报道用语，提示这是引用评估结论而非记者判断。

**逻辑关系**：结果 + 归因（依据年度评估）。

**参考译文**：根据科学家发布的一份重要而严峻的年度评估，人类赖以生存的行星生命支持系统，在持续污染与栖息地破坏中已恶化到有记录以来最差的水平。

**写作迁移**：*The transport networks relied on by millions have fallen to their lowest reliability on record, according to a regulatory review.* 根据一份监管评估，数百万人依赖的交通网络可靠性已降至有记录以来最低。

### 长列举与句末插入

**原句**：[3] The seven key indicators in the danger zone include climate change, change in biosphere integrity, land system change, freshwater change, modification of biogeochemical flows, introduction of novel entities and, for the first time last year, ocean acidification.

**主干**：主句 `The seven key indicators ... include ...`，后面是一串并列宾语。

**层次与易错点**：七个并列项由逗号分隔，最后一个前用 and；`for the first time last year` 插在 and 与最后一项之间，说明海洋酸化是新增项。易错点：插入语打断了 and 与 ocean acidification 的连接，容易漏读最后一项；`novel entities` 不是「小说实体」，而是新出现的化学物质与材料。另需注意列举里有几组是同构表达（change、modification、introduction），中文不必逐词对应，可按「变化、改变、引入」统一处理。

**逻辑关系**：总述 + 列举（最后一项带时间标记）。

**参考译文**：处于危险区的七项关键指标包括：气候变化、生物圈完整性变化、土地系统变化、淡水变化、生物地球化学循环的改变、新物质的引入，以及去年首次越界的海洋酸化。

**写作迁移**：*The report tracks four pressures: energy use, land conversion, water stress and, for the first time this year, noise pollution.* 该报告追踪四种压力：能源使用、土地转换、水资源紧张，以及今年首次纳入的噪声污染。

### 插入同位语与 although 让步

**原句**：[4] Only two of the assessed areas, stratospheric ozone depletion and atmospheric aerosol loading, have shown improvement over the last decade, although researchers warn that atmospheric aerosols, which can form air pollution, are still a problem in several regions of the world.

**主干**：主句 `Only two of the assessed areas ... have shown improvement`；although 从句作让步。

**层次与易错点**：两个破折号之间的内容（此处用逗号）是 areas 的同位语，说明是哪两项；although 从句里又套了 which 引导的非限定从句修饰 aerosols。易错点：同位语被逗号隔开，容易把 stratospheric ozone depletion 误读成主句主语的一部分；only two 与 although 连用，形成「略有改善，但仍有问题」的双重限定。

**逻辑关系**：让步——即便有改善，也不代表问题解决。

**参考译文**：在所评估的领域中，只有两项——平流层臭氧消耗与大气气溶胶负荷——在过去十年有所改善，尽管研究者警告说，可能形成空气污染的大气气溶胶在世界若干地区仍是问题。

**写作迁移**：*Only one of the measures, fuel standards, has produced clear results, although experts note that enforcement varies by region.* 这些措施中只有燃油标准一项成效明显，尽管专家指出执法力度因地区而异。

### 名词化主语与引语收尾

**原句**：[10] The ability of landscapes and bodies of water to cope with this and act as a buffer to the worsening climate crisis is "weakening and may be significantly overestimated", the new report warns.

**主干**：主语 `The ability of ... to cope with this and act as a buffer ...`，谓语是引语中的 `is weakening and may be significantly overestimated`。

**层次与易错点**：主语中心词是 ability，后接两个并列不定式 `to cope with` 与 `act as`；谓语被引号包住，句末 `the new report warns` 是倒装的引述语。易错点：`may be significantly overestimated` 说的是「这种能力可能被高估」，不是「能力正在高估」；引语提前会让读者一时找不到谓语。

**逻辑关系**：转折 + 警告。

**参考译文**：景观与水体应对这一切、并作为缓冲抵御日益恶化的气候危机的能力，正「在减弱，而且可能被显著高估」，这份新报告警告说。

**写作迁移**：*The capacity of local hospitals to absorb winter demand is "close to its limit", the review warns.* 该评估警告说，当地医院承接冬季需求的能力「已接近极限」。

## 术语与行业黑话

- **planetary boundaries**｜本文含义：界定地球系统安全范围的一组科学阈值｜使用背景：2009 年由 Rockström 等人提出，共九条，用于判断人类活动是否越界｜近词辨析：与 carbon budget（碳预算）不同，边界涵盖多个系统而非单一指标｜可否迁移到雅思：可（环境类大作文）。
- **safe operating space**｜本文含义：人类活动可以安全进行的范围｜使用背景：与行星边界配套的概念，越界即进入风险区｜近词辨析：它是科学框架内的范围，不是政策目标或法定限额｜可否迁移到雅思：可。
- **biosphere integrity**｜本文含义：生物圈完整性，衡量物种多样性与生态系统功能｜使用背景：边界体系中的核心指标之一，与物种灭绝速率相关｜近词辨析：与 biodiversity（生物多样性）相关但更宽，包含系统功能｜可否迁移到雅思：可（需解释）。
- **novel entities**｜本文含义：人类新合成的化学物质、塑料与材料｜使用背景：指自然界原本不存在的物质，难以降解且影响未知｜近词辨析：不是「新奇事物」的日常义，属专业术语｜可否迁移到雅思：可（需解释）。
- **green-hushing**｜本文含义：企业因顾虑而刻意淡化自身的环保表态｜使用背景：由 greenwashing（漂绿）反向构造，2020 年代随政治压力出现｜近词辨析：greenwashing 是说得太多太假，green-hushing 是说得太少｜可否迁移到雅思：可（商业与环境话题）。

## 背景补充

- **事实**：行星边界评估自 2009 年开始，今年是九条边界中七条同时越界，且程度为历年最高（[2]）。这是报道的核心数据。
- **事实**：分析由波茨坦气候影响研究所主持，60 位科学家参与（[5]）。这是该结论在科学界的来源与规模。
- **事实**：报告在纽约气候周开幕之际发布，主办方称参会者超过 10 万人，规模为历届最大（[16][17]）。这说明议题热度与政策行动之间存在落差。
- **观点（Helen Clarkson）**：她认为今年出现了前所未见的「绿色噤声」，许多企业到场却不显眼（[19]）。这是主办方观察，不是统计结论。
- **事实**：报告发布前一年内，巴基斯坦发生特大洪水，欧洲出现创纪录高温与野火，美国经历最热夏天，西藏—尼泊尔边境发生灾难性冰川洪水（[13]）。报道用这组事件说明「边界越界」并非抽象数据。

## 雅思迁移

**写作**

题目：*Some people think environmental problems should be solved by governments, while others believe individuals should take responsibility. Discuss both views and give your own opinion.*

提纲：
- 立场：两者都需要，但结构与规模决定政府必须承担主要责任。
- 主体一：个人行为能减少日常排放，也能形成社会压力。
- 主体二：能源、土地利用与工业排放超出个人能控制的范围，需要政策与基础设施。
- 结论：个人负责可行范围内的选择，政府负责制定规则与投资公共系统。

示范段（83 词）：

> Individuals can reduce their own emissions and, just as importantly, signal that change is expected. Yet the pressures described in scientific assessments come mainly from energy systems, land use and industrial production, none of which an individual can alter alone. A household that insulates its home still depends on how electricity is generated. Governments therefore carry the larger responsibility, while individuals decide how quickly public demand makes action politically possible.

**阅读**

1. 报告的核心结论是什么？
   定位：[2]
   依据："seven are being breached at their highest ever levels of transgression"
   答案：九条行星边界中有七条被突破，且突破程度为有记录以来最高。

2. 哪些领域有所改善，作者为何仍保留警惕？
   定位：[4]
   依据："although researchers warn that atmospheric aerosols ... are still a problem"
   答案：平流层臭氧消耗与大气气溶胶负荷有所改善，但气溶胶在若干地区仍构成空气污染问题。

3. 为什么说自然的缓冲能力可能被高估？
   定位：[10]
   依据："weakening and may be significantly overestimated"
   答案：森林与海洋虽然吸收了大量二氧化碳，但已有迹象显示这些系统在减弱，其承接能力可能没有原先估计的那么强。

4. 亚马逊雨林的例子说明了什么风险？
   定位：[11]
   依据："could reach a tipping point where it becomes a savannah"
   答案：说明关键生态系统可能越过临界点并发生不可逆转变，进而影响气候稳定、粮食与水安全以及人类健康。

5. 报道结尾揭示的矛盾是什么？
   定位：[17]–[19]
   依据："I've not seen this level of 'green-hushing' before"
   答案：气候周参会人数创纪录，但企业普遍不愿高调谈论气候问题，公众关注与实际行动之间存在落差。

**Part 3**

1. Why do some companies avoid talking about climate action?（出处：[19]）
   思路：讲政治压力、被指「漂绿」的风险与投资者反应。
   *Speaking loudly can attract **accusations of greenwashing**, and in some markets it now carries political cost. Many firms prefer to act quietly and let the results speak.*

2. Are scientists' warnings effective in changing public behaviour?（出处：[6]）
   思路：区分「知道」与「行动」，提信息疲劳与具体化。
   *They raise awareness, but awareness alone rarely changes habits. Warnings work better when they are tied to **visible local effects** and to policies that make the greener option the easier one.*

3. Should environmental data be presented with more urgency in the media?（出处：[20]）
   思路：承认紧迫感有帮助，但要区分事实与修辞。
   *Urgency can be appropriate when the evidence supports it, but constant alarm tends to **switch readers off**. The useful balance is precise data plus a clear explanation of what it means.*
